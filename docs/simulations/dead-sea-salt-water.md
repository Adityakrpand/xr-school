## XR fit justification

The learner repeats the same controlled egg trial in two transparent tanks, then changes only the amount of dissolved salt. Salt particles, a density meter, the egg's complete underwater path, and a floating human model make the invisible relationship between dissolved matter, density, and buoyant support spatial and observable. The experiment is repeatable without spills, preparation time, or restricted classroom visibility.

## Learning objective

Students will compare egg buoyancy in ordinary and sufficiently salty water and explain that dissolved salt increases water density, helping objects and people float more easily.

## Five-stage flow

1. **Explore the floating sea:** Inspect ordinary water, salty water, and salt crystals; predict which water helps an egg float.
2. **Compare two tanks:** Drop the same egg into ordinary and salty water and record its final position.
3. **Make salt water:** Add salt one spoonful at a time, stir to dissolve it, and retest until the egg floats.
4. **Density reveal:** See dissolved salt particles, the density meter, the floating egg, and a person supported by Dead Sea water.
5. **Scientist challenge:** Answer three concept questions, sort the egg observations, and earn the Junior Marine Scientist badge.

## Assessment hooks

- One pre-experiment prediction about ordinary versus salty water.
- Two controlled observations using the same egg.
- A repeatable salt-concentration experiment with an explicit dissolve step.
- One causal explanation check linking salt, density, and buoyant support.
- Three post-check questions and two observation classifications.

## Safety and accessibility

- Stationary VR camera; no artificial locomotion, forced submersion, flashes, or sudden cuts.
- Spoken narration is duplicated in the in-world cue card and browser control panel.
- Large text-labelled controls, high contrast, and `aria-live` feedback.
- Full browser and touch operation when WebXR is unavailable.
